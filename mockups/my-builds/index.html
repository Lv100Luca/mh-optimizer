My builds as a sketchpad: six directions

Today the tab is a scorer: you type a build in, it tells you what it does and compares it. The goal is to make it a place where you draft a build yourself: try an idea, see what it adds up to, fix the gaps, finish. Each page below pushes one direction as far as it goes so the differences are visible. The real answer is probably two of them together; see the bottom.

All pages show the same scenario: the "Current" great sword, a new "Crit draft" that wants Critical Boost 5, Weakness Exploit 5, Agitator 5, Maximum Might 3 and Counterstrike 3, with the head and chest picked so far. Pieces, sets and jewels are the real ones from data/*.json. Damage numbers are illustrative; the mockups cannot score. Status: round 1 reviewed 2026-10-16; 1 (without the source list), 2 and 4 picked; round 2 combines them; round 3 (current) makes the palette a drawer and gives targets a source

What makes drafting hard today

From the code (BuildsPanel.razor, EquipmentEditor.razor) and the running app.

1
You start from nothing.

"+ New" gives seven empty rows and a dropdown per row with hundreds of pieces sorted by rarity. The search finds skills, but you need to know what you want from each slot first.

2
The skill totals are not where you edit.

You design toward skills, but the sum (Agitator 2 of 5, Critical Boost at the cap) is on the build card at the very bottom and the targets in a section of their own. Free slots are nowhere.

3
Jewels are 18 pickers.

Every slot is filled by hand, one dropdown at a time. The optimizer knows how to fill slots and is not asked.

4
Sets are not a unit.

Hunters think "2 Bale for Dark Arts, 2 Udra"; the editor only knows single pieces and shows the bonus names in small print.

5
The sketch and the solver are not linked.

Results can lock a piece into Allowed armor. The reverse ("I chose these two, finish it for me") does not exist, although the lock mechanics are there.

6
The page is long.

Conditions, damage per attack, upgrades, compare and the card follow the editor. Drafting wants editor, totals and score in one view; analysis can fold.

Each card says what the page changes, what it costs to build, and which of the problems above it attacks (red tags). Open a page for the design notes.

How they combine

They are not exclusive. The sheet (1) and the table (6) are two shapes of the same thing: totals where you edit. The entry flows (3, 5) decide what the first screen of a new build is. Fill the rest (2) is a button that any of them can carry. The board (4) replaces the editor and is the only one that cannot be added on top.

Small: 1 + 2

Keep the editor, add the sheet beside it and the keep toggles with "Fill the rest". Two features, no new entry flow. The blank start is softened by the sheet's "where to get it" list and by the solver.

Guided: 3 + 1 + 2

A new build opens on the wishlist, picks land in the editor with the sheet, the solver closes the gaps. The most "sketch" of the combinations; the most UI too.

Hunter's way: 5 + 1

Sets first, then the sheet for jewels and single swaps. Best when you already know the set bonus you are after; weakest for pure skill-stacking.

Rebuild: 4 + 2

The board as the editor, the solver as its fill. A different app; worth it only if the current editor is considered a dead end.
